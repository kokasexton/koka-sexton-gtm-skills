# Hiring Signals

A job description is a company telling you, in writing, what problem it's trying to solve and with what tools.

## What to read in a posting

| In the JD | Signal |
|---|---|
| **The role** is in your function (e.g. first RevOps hire, 5 new SDRs) | The pain is about to scale |
| **"Build from scratch"** or **"first hire"** | Greenfield: the stack isn't chosen yet |
| **Tools named** ("experience with X") | Their stack, and whether you fit or replace it |
| **Goals named** ("own pipeline generation", "launch outbound") | The outcome to write to |
| **Many similar roles** | A growth push, with the budget behind it |

## Trajectory

- **Growing team** → scaling pain, process, and tooling: a strong signal.
- **Hiring a leader** → a new strategy is coming (see `skills/job-changes` once they start).
- **People leaving** (several departures in one team) → disruption; could be pain or a freeze. Handle with care.

## Play

Write to the **hiring manager or function leader**, about the outcome the role is for:

> Saw you're hiring five SDRs to launch outbound in EMEA. The ramp is usually the hard part. Here's the 30-day ramp plan a team your size used. Useful?

## Where to see it

Company careers pages, job boards, LinkedIn Jobs, and job-posting aggregators. Monitor ICP accounts for roles in your function.
