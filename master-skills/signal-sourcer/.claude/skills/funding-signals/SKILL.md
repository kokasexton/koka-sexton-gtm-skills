# Funding Signals

A raise means new budget and a plan the board expects executed. **The press release tells you the plan.** Read it.

## Read the round

| Look for | It tells you |
|---|---|
| **Stated use of funds** ("expand go-to-market", "enter Europe", "build AI") | Which function is about to grow, and which leader owns it |
| **Stage** | Early: founders still buy directly. Later: functional leaders own budgets. |
| **New investors or board members** | New expectations and new priorities |
| **Hiring after the round** | Confirms the plan (see `skills/hiring-signals`) |

## Timing (default)

Reach out **within the first few weeks**, while plans and vendor reviews are forming. After a quarter, the stack decisions are often made.

## Play

1. Pick the **leader whose function the money is for**, not the CEO by default.
2. Lead with **their plan**, not the money: "With the round earmarked for go-to-market, the SDR build-out is probably next…"
3. Offer something that helps them **execute faster**: a benchmark, a hiring plan, a teardown.

## Avoid

- "Congrats on the raise! Now that you have budget…" They've received fifty of these.
- Pitching before you understand what the money is for.

## Where to see it

Funding databases and newsletters, press releases, founder posts on LinkedIn, and investor portfolio pages.
