# Content Engineer

A content marketer produces content. A content engineer designs the system that produces, evaluates, distributes, and improves it. The loop is the job — measurement feeds back into source.

## The six layers

1. **Source** — where signal comes from: primary research, data pulls, engagement intelligence, customer conversations. Not "ideas."
2. **Knowledge** — brand facts made machine-legible: voice, positioning, proof points, do/don't lists, in a form the agent can retrieve.
3. **Generation** — LLM pipelines that respect voice and componentise output (hook, body, CTA as parts, not one blob).
4. **Evaluation** — verifiers and editorial gates *before* publish: voice check, slop check, fact pass. Nothing ships un-gated. Run `skills/content-evaluation`.
5. **Distribution** — channel systems and franchises, not one-off posts. Native formats. AEO/GEO for discovery (`master-skills/ai-search-visibility`).
6. **Measurement** — audience *quality*, not impressions. Who engaged, at what seniority, from which accounts.

## How to run it

Ask, in order: What's the source of truth? Is it structured for a machine? Does generation respect voice? What gate stops a bad post? How does it reach people? What tells us it worked?

The failure mode is always the same: a team that has generation and nothing else. Fix the loop where it's broken rather than adding posts.

## Benchmarks to use

- Cadence 3–5 posts/week, not daily (daily floods and cannibalises reach).
- Image formats outperform link posts ~2.8× in median engagement.
- 1–2 hashtags; 6+ costs ~63% of median engagement.
- Long beats short: 1,500+ char posts ≈ 2× the median of short ones.
- Comments are the signal: commenters are ~26% more senior than likers.
