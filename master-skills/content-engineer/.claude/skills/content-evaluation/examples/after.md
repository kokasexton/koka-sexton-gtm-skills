We analysed 2,279 LinkedIn posts from 246 creators. The people posting daily were not winning.

Posting 3–5 times a week produced a median of ~195 engagements per post — 2.4× the daily cohort. Push past 7 posts a week and the median collapses to ~80.

Why? Each post competes with your last one.

LinkedIn tests every post on a small cohort first. If that first group doesn't stop, read, and reply, the post never expands. Flood the feed and you split your own audience across five weaker posts instead of letting one strong one travel.

So the fix isn't "show up more." It's "show up better, less often":

→ Pick three days. Monday to Wednesday is the peak.
→ Write long. Posts over 1,500 characters earned about twice the median of short ones.
→ Use whitespace. Line breaks alone lifted engagement 72%.
→ Lead with an image or a document. Image posts beat link posts 2.8×.
→ Keep the link out of the body. Put it in the first comment.
→ Use one or two hashtags, not seven. Six or more cost 63% of median engagement.

And the part most people skip: reply to every comment.

Commenters were 26% more senior than likers in our data. 44% of them were Director or above. That isn't an audience metric. That's a pipeline metric.

The comment section is your CRM. The people who show up there twice are your warmest list in B2B — and almost nobody is working it.

What would change in your pipeline if you posted half as often and spent the saved hour replying to the people who already showed up?
