# Positioning: Alex Rivera

## ICP one-pager
- **Who (firmographic):** founders and first finance hires at seed to Series A software companies, a handful of people up to a small team, no full-time finance lead.
- **Situation (now):** a raise is coming within the next two quarters, or just closed, and the numbers live in a spreadsheet the founder built alone.
- **Behaviour:** the founder still does the books review, dreads the board finance update, and answers investor metric questions from memory.
- **The problem, in their words:** "I don't trust my own numbers," "investors keep asking for a metric I can't pull," "I'm doing finance at midnight."
- **Disqualifiers:** companies with a full finance team, pre-product ideas with no spend yet, anyone wanting bookkeeping only, anyone wanting a rubber stamp on a model they've already decided.

## Position, three versions
1. **Plain:** I help seed-stage founders get investor-ready numbers by setting up a simple finance backbone, so a raise doesn't stall on the data room.
   - Recognition: yes, the "investor-ready numbers" phrase is what they'd say. Relevance: tied to a raise. Range: narrow but holds a year of content.
2. **Sharper:** I help founders raising a seed or Series A stop answering metric questions from memory.
   - Recognition: strong. Relevance: strong. Range: slightly narrow; leaves out the post-raise board work.
3. **Repeatable:** I'm the finance person who gets a founder from spreadsheet to data room without hiring a CFO.
   - Recognition: strong. Relevance: strong. Range: wide enough, and a stranger could repeat it.

## Chosen: version 1, with the opening of version 3
Version 1 names the outcome and the moment. Trade-off: it leans on the raise, so Alex gives up the founder who isn't raising. That is the right loss for now.

## Words the buyer uses
data room, burn, runway, board update, investor metrics, month-end close, fractional CFO, spreadsheet.
