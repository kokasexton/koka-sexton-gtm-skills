# BTL Messaging (managers and practitioners)

Practitioners don't sign the contract, but they decide whether the evaluation happens. They care about time, tedium, and looking good to their boss.

## What they care about

- **The hour they lose every week** to a manual task.
- **The thing that breaks**: the report that's always wrong, the handoff that drops.
- **Their next review**: the metric their manager watches.

## The shape

1. **Observation about their work.** A post, a job description they'd recognise, a tool they use.
2. **The task-level pain.** Use their words, not your category's ("updating the lead list by hand on Fridays", not "data enrichment").
3. **Something useful now.** A template, a checklist, a teardown. It should be valuable even if they never reply.
4. **Low-commitment ask.** "Want the template?" beats "Can we hop on a call?"

Up to ~120 words is fine. Practitioners read more, but only if it's about their job.

## Arm the champion

If they reply, give them what they need to sell upward: the one-line business case, the number their VP cares about (see `skills/atl-messaging`), and an offer to join the internal conversation.

## Don't

- Pitch ROI. That's their boss's language.
- Go over their head in the same week you're emailing them.
- Assume their title tells you their pain. Read their posts and job descriptions.
