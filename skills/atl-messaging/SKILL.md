# ATL Messaging (VP / C-level / Director)

Executives buy outcomes and avoid risks. They don't read feature lists, and they forward emails that make them look smart to the person who'll do the work.

## What they care about

| Role | They're measured on | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| CEO / Founder | Growth, efficiency, focus | A market shift and what it means for the plan |
| CRO / VP Sales | Pipeline, win rate, forecast | Where pipeline is leaking, with evidence |
| CMO / VP Marketing | Pipeline contribution, CAC, brand | Audience quality over volume: who's actually engaging |
| CFO | Cost, risk, payback | Cost of the status quo, in their numbers |
| Director | Hitting the number the VP set | How peers solved the same constraint |

## The shape

1. **Observation at their altitude.** Name a public move: a hire, an expansion, a post they wrote, a reorg. Don't mention a tool.
2. **The implication.** One sentence on what it usually means for the metric they own.
3. **Proof, not claims.** One number from a source, or one named pattern you've seen.
4. **An opinion ask.** "Is this on your radar for H1?" or "Worth me sending the two-line version to your team?"

Keep it under ~80 words. Executives read on phones.

## Example

> Subject: Q1 pipeline from your posts
>
> Maria, your post on rebuilding the SDR team drew 40+ comments, and a good share came from Directors at accounts in your ICP.
>
> In the LinkedIn Algorithm Report data, commenters were 26% more senior than likers. Most teams never work that list.
>
> Worth me sending the 5 names I'd call first?

## Don't

- Use "quick question" or "just checking in".
- Ask for 30 minutes in touch one.
- Write like their subordinate or like a vendor. Write like a peer with a point of view.
