Most founders I work with open their finance tools once a month, when the books close.

By then the month is over. The decisions are made. The numbers are history.

So I ask for a different habit: three numbers, every Monday, ten minutes.

1. Cash in the bank, and the date it runs out at today's burn.
Say the date out loud. If you can't say it, you don't know your runway yet. You know your balance.

2. What came in last week versus what you forecast.
Not the whole forecast. Just the gap. A gap tells you which assumption is wrong before it compounds into next quarter.

3. Commitments that start next month.
Hires, contracts, tools, a lease. Founders feel revenue the day it lands. They rarely feel a commitment until the invoice arrives.

A small example. A founder I worked with kept putting off the third number. When we finally listed next month's commitments, two of them overlapped with a hire she hadn't mentioned in the plan. We moved the start date by three weeks. Nothing dramatic. Just a decision made on purpose instead of by invoice.

None of this needs a model. It needs a sheet with three cells and the discipline to open it.

When founders keep this up for a month, the same things tend to happen. The board update has fewer surprises. The hiring conversation gets shorter, because the runway date is already on the table. And the number stops feeling like a verdict and starts feeling like information.

I put the three cells into a one-page document. Swipe through it, then copy the structure into whatever you already use. A spreadsheet is fine. A napkin is fine.

The point is the Monday, not the tool.

Which of the three do you check least today?
